# Atomic findings (as of 2026-10-24)

Each item is a bounded claim, with evidence and a caveat. “Inference” is my synthesis, not a paper's own claim.

## Publication and task scope

- **F01 — Fact:** RLCoder is an ICSE 2025 paper; AlignCoder is an ASE 2025 paper. AlignCoder's arXiv v1 date is 2026-01-27, which is not its venue publication year. [S15](sources.csv) [S16](sources.csv)
- **F02 — Fact:** Several directly relevant 2026 works are now in official proceedings: LC² and AIRCoder at ACL, RepoShapley at Findings ACL, and ACAR at EMNLP. [S01](sources.csv)–[S04](sources.csv)
- **F03 — Fact:** “Repository-level” covers distinct tasks. ACAR and LC² evaluate FIM/line/API completion; RepoScope, AllianceCoder, InlineCoder, and the FSE context-engineering study focus largely on whole-function generation. Their numbers must not be ranked as if they were the same task. [S02](sources.csv) [S04](sources.csv) [S09](sources.csv)–[S12](sources.csv)

## What 2026 adds

- **F04 — Fact:** LC² separates a similarity-oriented retrieval representation from a later comprehension context, expanding retrieved material and optionally appending callee signatures/docstrings. Its paper's main comparison uses DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B, UniXcoder, top-5 retrieved candidates, and at most 4,096 cross-file tokens; on CrossCodeEval Python, fixed-token is 22.01 EM and LC² is 26.35. **Caveat:** exact prompt, chunking, decoding, and candidate pool still differ from this project, so these are not transferable expected scores. [S02](sources.csv)
- **F05 — Fact:** AIRCoder fuses eight signals spanning sparse/dense text, dependency existence, and structural hierarchy (path proximity, file/class/function similarity, and structural embedding). It reports 31.26 EM for DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B on CCEval Python; its training-free RRF variant is distinct from its learned MLP variant. [S01](sources.csv)
- **F06 — Fact:** AIRCoder's learned MLP uses pairwise MSE on the difference between generator-derived Edit Similarity labels. It therefore conflicts with the user's preference to avoid constructing pairwise training examples; its inference-time RRF variant avoids that learned loss. [S01](sources.csv)
- **F07 — Fact:** ACAR is a direct FIM retrieval system: lexical and AST/LSP structural rankings are fused at line level, followed by syntax-aware post-processing. It reports 42.98 EM on ReccEval and 32.86 on CrossCodeEval with Qwen2.5-Coder-3B, against 36.25/28.40 for the strongest cited retrieval baseline. The paper reports 87–95 ms non-decoding overhead under warm-cache conditions. **Caveat:** larger backbone, its own protocol, and warm-cache timing; compare methods by rerunning, not by table subtraction. [S04](sources.csv)
- **F08 — Fact:** RepoFuse explicitly separates “semantic context” from “similar context”: the former comes from lightweight static dependencies, the latter from code similarity. Its FSE 2026 paper is in the Industry Papers track and reports a production deployment at Ant Group, 156 ms average latency, an 18% acceptance-rate improvement, and CCEval EM improvements for Python/Java. **Caveat:** these deployment claims are author-reported from one company and a one-week A/B test; not a controlled public benchmark comparison. [S05](sources.csv)
- **F09 — Fact:** GrepRAG shows that cheap exact identifier search can be a strong repository-completion baseline. It improves naïve grep with identifier-weighted reranking and structure-aware deduplication; the paper reports 7.04–15.58% relative EM gain over its best baselines on CCEval. It also describes ambiguity, redundancy, and fragmented context as failure cases. [S06](sources.csv)
- **F10 — Fact:** RepoShapley treats context as a coalition: chunks can be complementary, redundant, or conflicting. Its offline process uses teacher-forced probes, a surrogate utility, Shapley-style values, and bounded generator verification, then distills keep/drop and retrieval control into a model. It uses K=10 in its default setup; exact subset evaluation scales as 2^K and its own limitations note that single-chunk probes can miss higher-order interactions. **Caveat:** reproducing the full method is expensive and it fine-tunes the generator/control model, so it is not a drop-in retriever-only recipe. [S03](sources.csv)
- **F11 — Fact:** SpecAgent moves exploration to repository indexing and prepares speculative contexts for likely future edits. It also explicitly identifies future-context leakage as a benchmark risk and introduces a synthetic benchmark. **Caveat:** its speculative future-edit setup is not equivalent to a left-context-only, one-retrieval completion benchmark. [S07](sources.csv)
- **F12 — Fact:** ArkRepoBench distinguishes Single-File, Cross-File Independent, and Cross-File Dependent examples, rather than treating any cross-file context as necessary. Its ArkTS results include cases where sparse Jaccard beats dense retrieval. **Caveat:** this is an ArkTS/HarmonyOS benchmark; the taxonomy transfers more safely than its leaderboard conclusions. [S08](sources.csv)
- **F13 — Fact:** RepoScope constructs a Repository Structural Semantic Graph, uses call-chain-aware four-view context, and reports static-analysis-only operation without additional training or multiple LLM queries. It evaluates repository-level full-function generation (CoderEval/DevEval), not prefix-only FIM; it is evidence for practical structural context, not direct evidence that its graph will improve this project's metric. [S09](sources.csv)
- **F14 — Fact:** AllianceCoder's study says useful in-context code and potential API information help while similar retrieved snippets can add noise; its proposed chain-of-thought/API method is tested on CoderEval and RepoExec function-generation tasks. This is a useful warning about noise, but not a direct FIM result. [S10](sources.csv)
- **F15 — Fact:** InlineCoder drafts an anchor and uses caller-side inlining plus callee retrieval along a call graph. That is a multi-stage whole-function-generation method, not a one-shot retriever for an existing left prefix. [S12](sources.csv)
- **F16 — Fact:** SabreCoder's structural sparse attention addresses long-context TTFT, not candidate retrieval. The paper reports 45–55% lower TTFT with accuracy within 3% on its settings; treat it as a separate generator/context-efficiency axis. [S13](sources.csv)
- **F17 — Fact:** CARET is an arXiv preprint as of this cutoff, not a verified peer-reviewed 2026 publication. It improves generation with sample routing and reverse-context likelihood; that latter signal needs code after the cursor, so it mismatches a left-only setup. [S14](sources.csv)

## Graphs and repository dependence

- **F18 — Fact:** Repository graph retrieval predates 2026: DraCo uses dataflow-guided repository context; GraphCoder uses a code-context graph for coarse-to-fine retrieval; RepoHyper uses search-expand-refine on a semantic graph. RepoScope adds another graph-based 2026 system. [S09](sources.csv) [S18](sources.csv)–[S20](sources.csv)
- **F19 — Inference:** The literature does **not** support “graphs are dead,” nor does it support “a graph alone is a contribution.” Newer papers generally combine graph/static structure with lexical/similarity evidence, context selection, or a specific task-facing mechanism. The defensible claim must be about how typed relations improve a controlled FIM retrieval decision, not merely that a graph was built. [S01](sources.csv) [S04](sources.csv)–[S06](sources.csv) [S09](sources.csv) [S18](sources.csv)–[S20](sources.csv)
- **F20 — Inference:** RLCoder and AlignCoder improve target-aware retrieval behavior, but their central contribution is not an explicit typed repository-dependency representation. AlignCoder uses generated completions to enhance the retrieval query and trains a retriever with RL; RLCoder uses generation feedback to train retrieval. By contrast, the 2026 structural approaches make dependency or hierarchy evidence explicit. [S01](sources.csv) [S15](sources.csv) [S16](sources.csv)
- **F21 — Inference:** AST chunk boundaries preserve local syntax better than arbitrary line splits, but flat chunk vectors do not themselves encode cross-file edges, API identity, caller/callee paths, or whether a target truly requires another file. Chunk representation and repository relation modeling are separate design axes. LC² and GrepRAG support the chunk-integrity/fragmentation part; AIRCoder, RepoFuse, RepoScope, and ArkRepoBench support the relation/necessity part. [S01](sources.csv)–[S02](sources.csv) [S05](sources.csv)–[S09](sources.csv)

## Evidence quality

- **F22 — Fact:** Published numbers use different backbones, retrieval budgets, chunking, context lengths, decoding, datasets, and cache conditions. EM/ES improvements in one paper are not a valid numerical ranking against this project's logs without a controlled rerun. [S01](sources.csv)–[S08](sources.csv)
- **F23 — Inference:** The strongest evidence-backed next step is a single logical retrieval pass over one frozen candidate pool, with explicit candidate roles (direct dependency/API/type, same-file structural context, analogous example) and relation-aware reranking. This preserves the one-retrieval constraint while testing repository dependence directly. It remains a hypothesis until evaluated against fixed-chunk/no-relation, lexical, dense, and hybrid controls.
